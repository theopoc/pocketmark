# Reader, highlights and notes

Documentary research, 30 October 2026. Scope: a personal saved-page reader on desktop and mobile. Sources are official product documentation, not hands-on testing or comparative usability studies. Recommendations below are design inferences for Pocketmark, informed by [PRODUCT.md](../PRODUCT.md), [DESIGN.md](../DESIGN.md) and the owner's rejection of the first four-tab reader and repeated large title.

**Owner's governing priority:** “pouvoir lire l'article directement ou si c'est trop compliqué, avoir le raccourci pour ouvrir directement l'URL originale.” Open the article directly when available; keep a prominent, always-visible “Open original” shortcut for web bookmarks. Annotation tools are optional companions, not the center of the reader.

## Documented examples

| Product | Documented behavior | Relevant distinction |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Readwise Reader | A selected highlight can receive a note; with sufficient width, annotations appear in the right margin. Document notes live in the right sidebar's Notebook tab; mobile opens Notebook through the top-right information button. Unmatched highlights remain available in Notebook even when their location cannot be matched. [Highlights, Tags, and Notes](https://docs.readwise.io/reader/docs/faqs/highlights-tags-notes) | Reading and annotation coexist; a document note has a different scope from a highlight note. The notebook remains a fallback when an anchor fails. |
| Readwise Reader | Readers can choose which side panels are visible by default, including hiding them. [Navigation](https://docs.readwise.io/reader/docs/faqs/navigation) | A secondary pane need not be permanently visible. |
| Zotero | Text selection opens a contextual highlight/underline color popup. A Notes button opens a note pane; annotations from the document or left annotation sidebar can be added to a note. An annotation included in a note offers “Show on Page” to recover its source context. [PDF Reader and Note Editor](https://www.zotero.org/support/pdf_reader) | Highlighting, reviewing annotations and writing a synthesis are related actions, rather than four equivalent full-page destinations. Zotero's multi-note citation workflow is broader than Pocketmark's needs. |
| Hypothesis | Selecting text offers Highlight or Annotate. Existing highlights open annotations; the sidebar presents quote and note together. Page notes are explicitly independent of selected text and have a separate view within the sidebar. [Annotation Basics](https://web.hypothes.is/help/annotation-basics/) | A plain highlight, a note attached to a passage and a general document note are distinct concepts. Collaboration controls in this product do not belong in Pocketmark. |

These examples establish plausible interaction patterns. They do not establish that sidebars outperform tabs, that a particular width is optimal, or that the products' current interfaces work well on Pocketmark's supported browsers.

## Options and tradeoffs — Pocketmark inference

| Pattern | Benefit | Cost and appropriate use |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Top-level Text / Highlights / Files / Notes tabs | Simple, full-width panels; straightforward on narrow screens. | Hides the source while reviewing or writing; switching becomes necessary for tightly related tasks. Gives files equal prominence to reading. The owner's feedback already rejects this arrangement. |
| Optional desktop annotation pane beside the article | Keeps the source and personal thinking visible together; supports reviewing several annotations. | Consumes horizontal space and introduces a second scroll region. Open only when enough width remains for readable article text; avoid squeezing the article to preserve the pane. |
| Contextual editor on selection or highlight activation | Keeps the note's scope and source clear; avoids navigating away to annotate. | Popovers can obscure text, collide with touch selection and hide other annotations. Use a compact action menu followed by an editor in the pane or mobile annotation view; do not rely on hover. |
| Mobile annotation view or sheet | Gives quotes and an editor usable width while keeping reading controls compact. | Temporarily covers the article. Explicit return must restore the same reading location; jumping to a passage must close the view and reveal its context. This is a proposed responsive adaptation, not a claim about the cited products. |

## Recommended direction — Pocketmark inference

1. **Open directly on the article.** Show the article title once in the content, followed by restrained source metadata. Keep the persistent toolbar short: Back/Close, prominent “Open original”, a secondary “Notes” action with annotation count, and overflow actions. Keep “Open original” visible on desktop and mobile, including unavailable or incomplete saved-content states; do not hide it in overflow or make users wait for capture. For standalone files without a website URL, omit this action. Do not repeat a large bookmark title above the article.
2. **Treat notes as an optional companion to reading.** Keep them closed initially. On sufficiently wide screens, “Notes” opens a dismissible right pane. On mobile and narrow desktop windows, use a full-width annotation view with a clear “Back to reading” action. Determine the transition from the space required by both columns rather than device names. Preserve article scroll, active highlight and drafts across opening, closing and resizing.
3. **Keep two scopes explicit inside that companion.** Put a clearly labeled “Document note” section beside a “Highlights” list. Each highlight contains its quote, saved color, optional attached note and “Add note” or “Edit note” action. The note stays attached to its quote; do not mix it into the general note field. An existing document note gets a readable preview and an explicit Edit action; an empty one gets a modest Add action rather than a permanently large blank editor. These can be sections or local disclosures, without restoring the rejected top-level tabs.
4. **Connect the list and article in both directions.** Activating a highlight reveals its note/editor; “Show in text” on a list entry returns to the anchored passage. Keep a quoted highlight visible in the list when its location cannot be recovered, and explain that limitation. Never imply that a quote located by a partial text match is certainly the original passage.
5. **Move files to a secondary action.** Expose available saved representations through “Saved files” or a compact view menu. Select the preferred readable representation without making users navigate a Files tab first. Retain explicit capture/unavailable states and access to existing assets; do not imply that every attachment supports formatted reading or highlighting.
6. **Keep editing predictable.** Retain Pocketmark's explicit save behavior and draft recovery. Save/cancel feedback belongs with the editor and must remain visible on phones when the keyboard is open. All actions need labels or accessible names, keyboard operation and visible focus. Avoid adding auto-highlighting merely because other products offer it: selecting text to copy must remain practical.

## Existing capture capabilities and limits — code inspection

[reader.go](../internal/capture/reader.go) already renders passive structural HTML, preserving headings, paragraphs, lists, tables, code and safe absolute HTTP(S) links. It strips active elements, styles and remote resources; its input is limited to 2 MiB and rendered output to 1 MiB. This is a bounded HTML filter, not a scored main-article extraction algorithm, and does not execute JavaScript. `ExtractText` delegates to the plain-text extractor in [capture.go](../internal/capture/capture.go); plain text and structured HTML are separate outputs, not proof that the full article was captured. Nearby [layout tests](../internal/capture/layout_test.go) cover malformed-navigation recovery and excluded content; [offline tests](../internal/capture/offline_test.go) cover structural preservation and removal of active content. These tests were read, not rerun for this document.

[Archive boundaries](ARCHIVES.md) specify that uploaded HTML is preferred over basic network capture, larger saved files remain downloadable, JavaScript applications need a browser-produced snapshot, and PDF/image OCR is absent. Therefore, direct reading is already technically possible for suitable saved HTML; complete or clean extraction from every site is unproven. Preserve “Open original” as a direct escape when capture is absent, fails or is unsatisfactory, rather than adding a resident browser or presenting annotations as substitute content.

## Validation and limits

This research changes no behavior. First verify immediate reading and one-action opening of the original URL, including absent, failed or incomplete captures. Then check optional annotation tasks with disposable data: add a highlight note, distinguish it from the document note, jump to source context, recover an unmatched highlight, open a saved file, and return without losing position or a draft. Check long content, narrow widths, keyboard navigation, touch selection and the on-screen keyboard.

The examples are not an implementation specification. They do not justify collaboration, citation management, native mobile apps, extra resident services, or new dependencies. A responsive pane and contextual controls fit the embedded browser UI; their actual accessibility and performance require implementation verification. Physical-phone, Firefox and Safari qualification remains subject to the repository's existing evidence boundaries.
